# America's Data Index — Risk Evaluation Rubric

This rubric provides a framework for assessing risk to public datasets maintained by federal statistical agencies.

## Dimensions

### 1. Political Salience
How politically visible or contentious is this dataset?
- **Low**: Technical dataset with limited public awareness
- **Medium**: Referenced in policy debates but not a focal point
- **High**: Frequently cited in partisan political arguments

### 2. Funding Stability
How stable is the funding for this dataset's collection and maintenance?
- **Low**: Dedicated, multi-year appropriation with bipartisan support
- **Medium**: Annual appropriation, historically stable but not guaranteed
- **High**: Subject to frequent budget debates or recent cuts

### 3. Staffing Continuity
How stable is the expert workforce maintaining this dataset?
- **Low**: Large, established team with institutional knowledge
- **Medium**: Some turnover but core expertise retained
- **High**: Key personnel departures, hiring freezes, or reorganization

### 4. Methodology Transparency
How well-documented and publicly accessible is the methodology?
- **Low**: Comprehensive public documentation, peer-reviewed methods
- **Medium**: Basic documentation available, some gaps
- **High**: Opaque methods, documentation missing or outdated

### 5. Downstream Dependencies
How many systems, analyses, or decisions depend on this dataset?
- **Low**: Niche use cases, limited downstream impact
- **Medium**: Used in multiple federal programs or academic research
- **High**: Critical infrastructure — billions in funding allocation, regulatory compliance

### 6. Update Recency
How current is the dataset relative to its expected update schedule?
- **Low**: On schedule, most recent release within expected window
- **Medium**: Slightly delayed (1-2 cycles behind)
- **High**: Significantly overdue, unexplained gaps, or announced discontinuation

### 7. Replication Difficulty
If this dataset were discontinued, how hard would it be to recreate?
- **Low**: Based on readily available inputs, could be reconstructed
- **Medium**: Requires specialized collection infrastructure, expensive but possible
- **High**: Irreplaceable — depends on longitudinal observation, unique access, or institutional relationships
